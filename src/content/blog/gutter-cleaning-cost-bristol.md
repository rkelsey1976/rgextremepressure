If you've searched for gutter cleaning prices in Bristol, you'll have noticed that hardly anyone gives a straight answer. That's because the price really does depend on the house. Two properties on the same street can need very different amounts of work.

Here's what actually moves the price, a rough idea of what's typical, and how to get a quote that holds up.

## A Rough Guide to Typical Prices

These are broad ranges for the wider UK market, not an RG Extreme Pressure price list. For a standard gutter clear on a house that isn't badly neglected, you'd usually expect to see something like:

- **Small terrace or flat-fronted house:** roughly £50 to £100
- **Semi-detached:** roughly £80 to £150
- **Detached or larger family home:** roughly £120 to £250 or more

Tall, awkward or heavily blocked properties can sit above those figures. A quote well below the range is worth questioning, and so is one well above it with no explanation. We give free quotes because we'd rather price your actual house than guess from a postcode.

## What Affects the Price

### House height and number of storeys

A bungalow is quick. A three-storey Victorian terrace takes longer, and the equipment and method matter more. We clear gutters with the SkyVac ground-based vacuum system and by hand, which means nobody needs to climb a ladder or scaffold for most jobs. Height still affects the time involved, though, and so the price.

### Length of the gutter run

This is about how much gutter there is, not just how big the house looks. Extensions, porches, garages and dormers all add runs. A modest-looking house with a side extension and a garage can have nearly double the guttering you'd guess from the front.

### Access

Bristol is full of hillside streets, tight terraces and houses with narrow side passages. Things that slow a job down include:

- Steep or terraced gardens with uneven ground
- Conservatories or lean-tos sitting under the gutter line
- Parked cars or bins blocking a drive
- Gates that are locked or too narrow to get through

Good access keeps the price down. If something might get in the way, mention it when you ask for a quote.

### How blocked the gutters are

This is the biggest variable. Gutters that were cleared last winter and have a light layer of leaves and grit are quick. Gutters that haven't been touched for years are different. Compacted wet leaves turn into a dense, mossy sludge, often with seedlings growing out of it, and that takes much longer to clear properly. If you can see plants growing along the gutter line, expect the job to be towards the higher end.

### Downpipes

Clearing the gutter is only half the job if the downpipe is still blocked. Water has to get away somewhere. If a downpipe is jammed with debris, it takes extra time to clear and flush through. It's worth asking whether downpipes are included in the quote rather than assuming.

### Extras

Some people want more than a clear-out. Common add-ons include:

- Cleaning the fascias and the outside face of the gutters
- [Roof moss removal](/roof-cleaning-bristol/), which is often worth doing at the same time because moss and debris wash straight into the gutters
- Solar panel cleaning, particularly where birds have been nesting underneath (see our post on [birds under solar panels](/blog/birds-under-solar-panels-winter-gutter-problem/))

Combining jobs in one visit can reduce the total cost compared with booking them separately.

## Why Winter Timing Matters

The most important gutter clear of the year is after the leaves have fully come down, which in this area usually means November and December. Clear them too early and the next few weeks of leaf drop fill them again, so you've paid for a job that only lasted a fortnight.

The practical tip: **wait until the trees are mostly bare, then book your clear so the gutters and downpipes are ready for the heavy winter rain.** Our post on [autumn leaves blocking downpipes](/blog/autumn-leaves-blocking-downpipes/) covers why downpipes are often the weak point.

If you're not sure whether yours are due, these are the usual signs:

- Water spilling over the front of the gutter in heavy rain
- Plants or moss growing along the gutter
- Damp patches or tide marks on walls and fascias
- A gutter run that's sagging under the weight

There's more detail in our guide on [how to tell if your gutters need cleaning](/blog/how-to-tell-if-gutters-need-cleaning/).

The reason to bother is that overflowing water doesn't stay in the gutter. It soaks the fascias and runs down the walls, and over a wet winter that causes damp and staining that costs far more to put right than a gutter clear.

## Why Busy Months Cost You in Other Ways

We can't promise specific availability, but it's a fact of the trade that demand climbs once the leaves are down and the first big storms arrive. Booking a few weeks ahead usually gives you more choice of dates and less of a rush.

## How to Get a Quote You Can Compare

When you're comparing prices, ask each company the same questions:

1. **Is the price fixed for the whole house**, or per hour or per metre?
2. **Are downpipes included?**
3. **How will the gutters be cleared?** From the ground, from a ladder, or from scaffold?
4. **Are they insured?** Ask for proof, especially for anyone working at height.
5. **Will you see evidence the job was done?** Before and after photos are a reasonable thing to ask for.

A lower quote isn't automatically better. Gutters that are only skimmed from the top, with the downpipes left blocked, will fill up again quickly and you'll be paying twice.

## How Often Do You Need to Do It?

For most homes, once a year after the leaves have fallen is enough. Houses under or near trees, or with solar panels that attract nesting birds, may need a second check in spring or summer. If your gutters are clear and the water's running away properly, you don't need to do anything extra.
