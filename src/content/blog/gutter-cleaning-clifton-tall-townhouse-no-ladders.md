If you own one of Clifton's tall Georgian or Victorian houses, you've probably looked up at the gutters and wondered how anyone gets to them. Three or four storeys, a lower-ground floor, a steep street and a car parked right outside don't make for easy access. The usual answer is a scaffold tower or a very long ladder, and neither is cheap or particularly pleasant to think about.

The good news is that many Clifton gutters can be cleared entirely from the ground. Here's how that works, where it stops working, and why the next few weeks are the right time to book.

## How a Ground-Based Clear Works

We clear gutters with the SkyVac, a ground-based vacuum system. It uses a powerful vacuum unit at the bottom and a set of lightweight extension poles that reach up to the gutter. Debris is sucked down a hose and into a collection unit, so leaves, moss, silt and seeds come out of the gutter and don't get dropped onto your front steps, your car or your neighbour's railings.

Nobody goes up a ladder and nobody has to climb onto the roof. For a tall house on a narrow pavement, that matters. There's no ladder footing to worry about on a sloping street, and no scaffold licence or road closure to arrange.

We also work by hand where it makes sense. Lower gutters on bay windows, porches, rear extensions and garden-level outbuildings are often easier to clear directly, and some compacted debris needs loosening before it will come out.

## What It Can Reach on a Clifton House

In most cases, the SkyVac handles the main run of gutter along the front and rear of a typical terrace or villa. It copes well with:

- **Standard half-round and ogee gutters** in cast iron, aluminium or plastic
- **Gutters that are packed with leaves, moss and silt**, which is the most common problem after autumn
- **Hopper heads and the tops of downpipes**, where debris tends to bunch up and block the flow
- **Awkward positions**, such as gutters behind a front garden wall, over a basement area or above a shared pathway

The system uses extension poles, so there is a limit to how high it will go. A very tall frontage with a deep lower-ground floor on a sloping street can sit at the edge of what's practical from the ground. We'd rather tell you that at the quote stage than discover it halfway up the house. Photos of the property often make this easy to judge, or we can take a look in person.

## What It Can't Do

This is the part many gutter-cleaning pages skip. A ground-based clear is not the answer to everything on an older house:

### Parapet and box gutters

Many Georgian terraces have gutters hidden behind a parapet wall, or lead-lined channels set into the roof structure. If you can't see the gutter from the street, we usually can't clear it from the ground either. These generally need roof access, and often a roofer who works with lead.

### Leaks and repairs

Clearing a gutter does not fix a cracked cast-iron joint, a sagging bracket or a rusted-through section. If water has been escaping for years, cleaning will show you where the problem is, but it won't solve it. On older houses, a clean sometimes shows that a repair is due, and that job may well need scaffold.

### Blockages deep in the downpipe

Long downpipes on tall houses can have blockages well below the gutter, at bends or where they enter the drain. The SkyVac deals with what's at the top and around the outlet. A blockage further down may need rodding or a drainage specialist.

### Gutters you can't see into

From the ground, you judge the job by what comes out and by how the gutter runs once it rains. If a gutter has been neglected for years, or a downpipe is blocked far down, a single visit may not tell the full story. We'll say so if we think more work is needed.

## Signs Your Clifton Gutters Need Clearing

On a tall house it's easy to miss the problem until water is coming through a wall. Look out for:

- Water spilling over the front of the gutter in heavy rain
- Plants, moss or even small saplings growing along the gutter line
- Damp patches or dark tide marks on the walls or fascias below
- A sagging run, where the weight of debris is pulling the gutter away from its fixings

If you spot any of these, our guide on [how to tell if gutters need cleaning](/blog/how-to-tell-if-gutters-need-cleaning/) goes through them in more detail. Left alone, overflowing water soaks fascias and brickwork, and on older lime-mortared walls that's a slow but real problem.

## Why Book After the Leaves Fall and Before Winter Rain

It's tempting to clear gutters as soon as the first leaves land, but that often means doing it twice. The most useful clear of the year is after the leaves have mostly come down, which in practice means November and December.

Clifton has plenty of mature trees in gardens and along streets, and they can keep dropping well into the season. If you clear in early October, you may find the gutter full again a month later. Waiting until the trees are largely bare means one visit sets you up for the wet months ahead.

The flip side is not leaving it too late. Winter rain, and any frost on top of it, is when blocked gutters and downpipes do their damage. Overflow down a front elevation can soak the wall for weeks, and frozen water in a blocked gutter adds weight and strain to old brackets. So the sensible window is once the leaves are down and before the worst of the weather arrives. Our post on [autumn leaves blocking downpipes](/blog/autumn-leaves-blocking-downpipes/) explains why the downpipes deserve as much attention as the gutters themselves.

### A practical tip

Before you book, walk down the street on a dry afternoon and look up at your gutter line from the pavement opposite. Note any sections that look sagging, rusty at the joints or covered in green growth. Photos of those spots help us judge what's reachable and what may need a separate repair.

## What About Jet Washing in Clifton?

We get asked about this a lot, usually about front steps, paths and basement areas that have gone green and slippery. Wet leaves on top of that make things worse. These surfaces can still be cleaned in a mild spell, but jet washing porous surfaces is best avoided at 4°C or below, or with a frost forecast within 24 hours. We time jobs around the forecast, so don't be surprised if we move a day.
