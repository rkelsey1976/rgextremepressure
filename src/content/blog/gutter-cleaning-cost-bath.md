If you've searched for what gutter cleaning costs in Bath, you've probably noticed that most firms don't put a price on their website. We don't either, and that isn't to be cagey. A two-bedroom terrace and a four-storey stone townhouse are different jobs, and any figure we printed would be wrong for one of them.

What we can do is explain what moves the price up or down, so you know what a fair quote looks like and what to tell whoever you ask.

## What Actually Decides the Price

Five things matter, roughly in this order.

### 1. House height

A two-storey semi is quicker to clear than a three- or four-storey Georgian townhouse. Height changes the equipment, the setup time and how carefully each section has to be worked. Bath has plenty of tall, narrow houses, so this is often the biggest factor.

### 2. Access

We clear gutters from the ground with the SkyVac vacuum system, and by hand where needed. That means no ladders propped against your fascia, but we still need to get near the walls. Things that affect this:

- **Side or rear access.** Many terraces have no side passage, so the back gutters mean working through the house or along a shared alley.
- **Sloping gardens.** Bath is hilly, and a steep or terraced garden slows things down.
- **Parked cars, planters and conservatories** in the way of the working area.

### 3. How blocked the gutters are

A gutter cleared every year is mostly a light vacuum job. One that hasn't been touched for five years can hold compacted, rotted leaf mulch, moss, silt from roof tiles and the odd tennis ball. It takes longer, and it's usually where we find plants growing along the run.

### 4. Length of run and number of downpipes

More gutter means more time. A long detached or L-shaped house costs more than a short terrace frontage. Downpipes matter too, because a clear gutter that drains into a blocked pipe still overflows. We check that water runs freely at the bottom as well.

### 5. Extensions, conservatories and lower roofs

Rear extensions, porches, bay windows and conservatories each add their own short runs of gutter, often at awkward angles and often collecting the most debris, because they sit below the main roof. They're easy to forget when you're thinking about the house as a whole, so mention them when you ask for a quote.

## How This Plays Out on Different Bath Properties

**Terraces.** Usually a long, continuous run at the front and back, and access is the main variable. Shared runs between neighbours can also complicate things, and it's worth checking who is responsible for what.

**Semis.** Usually the most straightforward: two or three storeys, side access, clear working space. Extensions are the usual add-on.

**Stone townhouses.** The most variable. Height is the obvious factor, but some older Bath properties have parapet or concealed gutters behind the stonework, or cast iron that needs gentle handling. Not every gutter on every period building can be cleared from the ground, and we'd rather tell you that at the quote stage than discover it on the day.

## Why the Post-Leaf-Fall Clear Is the One to Pay For

If you're only going to have your gutters done once a year, make it **November or December, once the leaves are mostly down**.

Gutters cleared in early autumn can refill within weeks if you have trees nearby. A clear after the leaves have fallen means the gutters go into the wettest months empty. Winter is also when a blocked gutter does the most harm. Heavy rain spills over the front and soaks the fascia and walls, and over time that can mean damp patches, tide marks and rot in the timber.

If trees overhang your roof and you want to fit in an extra clean, spring is the sensible second visit. But if you pick one, pick the one after the leaves have gone.

Not sure whether yours need doing? Look for:

- Water pouring over the front of the gutter in heavy rain
- Plants or moss growing in the gutter
- Damp patches or tide marks on walls and fascias
- A gutter that sags in the middle

## Getting a Quote That's Easy to Compare

Whoever you ask, a few details make the number accurate:

- How many storeys the house has
- Whether it's a terrace, semi, detached or townhouse
- Any extensions, conservatories or porches
- Whether you can get access at the back and sides
- When the gutters were last cleared, if you know
- A couple of photos, if you can, of the front and back

When comparing quotes, check what's included. Does it cover downpipes? Is the firm insured? Will they check the gutters are flowing at the end? Pricing that looks cheap often leaves things out.
